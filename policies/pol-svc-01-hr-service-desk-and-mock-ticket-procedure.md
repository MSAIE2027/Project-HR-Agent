# HR Service Desk and Mock Ticket Procedure

## Request categories

The fictional service desk accepts policy questions, record corrections, benefits enquiries, leave support, equipment coordination and sensitive HR referrals. An urgent safety matter uses the emergency route rather than a routine ticket. The category should reflect the user's need without placing unnecessary sensitive detail in the summary.

## Ticket creation

A ticket tool requires a valid synthetic employee ID, category, concise summary and explicit user confirmation. The generated ID proves only that a local demonstration record was written. It does not contact a production service desk, send a notification or create a legal record. The response must state that boundary clearly.

## Triage and ownership

Routine matters are assigned to the appropriate fictional queue. Sensitive matters are restricted to authorised HR. A ticket that lacks essential information is returned for clarification. The assistant may prepare a summary but must not decide the outcome of a complaint or exception request.

## Closure and learning

A ticket is closed only when the owner records the outcome and any follow-up. Trends may be reviewed to improve policies and workflows. Evaluation tickets used for testing are excluded from operational reporting.

## Purpose and scope

This fictional policy forms part of MSAIE Group's demonstration HR governance framework. It applies only to the synthetic employees, managers, locations, balances and actions included in this educational project. The policy is designed to support consistent decisions, provide clear approval paths and preserve an auditable record. It does not create employment rights, replace local law or authorise the assistant to make a final legal, medical, tax, immigration or disciplinary determination. Where this policy conflicts with a mandatory local requirement, the matter must be escalated to an authorised HR professional before action is taken.

## Roles and accountability

Employees are responsible for giving complete and accurate information, reading the applicable procedure and obtaining required approvals before relying on an outcome. Managers are responsible for operational coverage, consistency and timely decisions. HR owns policy interpretation and exception records. Information Security owns technical access controls. Finance owns expense controls. Legal, tax and immigration specialists provide advice when a request crosses their areas. The MSAIE assistant may retrieve evidence, call approved tools, identify missing information and prepare fictional drafts, but it must not silently bypass an approval or claim that a provisional result is final.

## Records and privacy

Only the minimum information needed for the stated purpose should be collected. Records must be stored in the approved synthetic system, labelled with the employee ID, action type, date and decision owner, and retained according to the records policy. Sensitive information must not be copied into free-text fields unless necessary. Access is role based and logs should show which tool was called, which arguments were supplied and what structured result was returned. Hidden chain-of-thought is not a business record and must not be exposed; concise operational traces are sufficient.

## Exceptions and escalation

An exception requires a documented business reason, a named decision owner, a defined duration and the approvals listed in the manager approval matrix. A request must be escalated when facts are missing, evidence conflicts, a sensitive allegation is raised, the employee record cannot be found, the requested action would create a real external effect, or the policy corpus does not support a reliable answer. The assistant should say what is missing and should not fill gaps with invented facts. Emergency situations should use the company's emergency channels rather than the normal request workflow.

## Review and assurance

HR reviews this fictional policy set annually and after any material incident or workflow failure. Quality checks sample citations, tool selections, confirmation gates and action logs. Findings are converted into corrective actions with an owner and target date. Evaluation results should include groundedness, citation accuracy, workflow completion, escalation accuracy, action safety and latency. Changes to a policy require version control, approval, an effective date and re-indexing of the retrieval store so that superseded text is not presented as current guidance.

## Intake and minimum information

A service request states the outcome the employee needs, the relevant policy or system, the requested date and a concise issue summary. Include a valid synthetic employee ID only when an employee-specific lookup or ticket requires it. Do not ask for passwords, payment security codes, medical history or unrelated family information. Sensitive allegations belong in the confidential HR route, not a routine service category. The assistant may ask a short clarifying question when the category or essential fields are missing. If the user cannot provide the detail, explain the correct human channel rather than creating a ticket that implies the issue is fully understood.

## Triage and routing

Route routine questions to the policy owner or service queue associated with the request. Benefits status and balance questions use structured employee tools; general policy questions use cited retrieval; equipment faults use IT support; safety incidents use the urgent channel. Harassment, discrimination, retaliation and legal concerns go to authorised HR without investigation by the assistant. A missing employee record or ambiguous classification should be returned for clarification or HR review. Priority follows documented service criteria and immediate safety risk, not persuasive language or seniority. A local mock ticket is not a production case or notification.

## Ticket state and ownership

An accepted ticket has a synthetic identifier, category, summary, requester, owner and current status. The owner records whether information is needed, who will follow up and the next expected action. A request can remain pending while an authorised person reviews it; the assistant must not change that state based on conversational assumptions. Reassignment records the reason and new owner. A duplicate may be linked to an existing ticket after the service owner checks it. The assistant may report fields returned by the tool but should not invent a deadline, queue or resolution absent from the structured result.

## Confirmation, closure and learning

Before creating a local demonstration ticket, show the proposed category and concise summary and ask for explicit confirmation. The user can correct or cancel the draft first. Confirmation authorises only that fictional local record, not a real message, production write or external notification. After creation, state the generated ID and local-only boundary plainly. An owner closes a ticket only after recording the outcome and follow-up; a request that received no response or was redirected is not resolved. Trends may be reviewed using de-identified categories to improve policy wording and routing. Evaluation fixtures stay separate from operational reporting, and the trace records tools and confirmation without exposing hidden reasoning.
