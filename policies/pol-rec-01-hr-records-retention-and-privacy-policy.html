HR Records Retention and Privacy Policy

Data minimisation

HR collects only information that is relevant to a defined employment purpose. Free-text fields should not contain unnecessary medical, financial or family detail. Synthetic demonstration data is clearly labelled and must never be confused with a real employee record. The assistant should answer using the minimum fields required.

Access and correction

Access is role based, logged and reviewed. Employees may request correction of inaccurate personal information through the authorised process. A tool lookup is not proof that a real record is complete. The assistant should state when a record is missing or when its source is synthetic.

Retention and disposal

Different record categories have approved retention periods. Records are held longer only when required for a legal hold, investigation or mandatory obligation. Disposal must be secure and documented. Local temporary files created by the demonstration, such as mock-action logs and regenerated vector indexes, contain only synthetic information and may be cleared during a cold start.

AI and audit trails

AI-assisted workflows retain citations, tool names, validated arguments, structured outcomes and confirmation decisions. They do not retain hidden reasoning. Logs should support reconstruction of what the system did without exposing secrets. Evaluation data must not contain real private information.

Purpose and scope

This fictional policy forms part of MSAIE Group's demonstration HR governance framework. It applies only to the synthetic employees, managers, locations, balances and actions included in this educational project. The policy is designed to support consistent decisions, provide clear approval paths and preserve an auditable record. It does not create employment rights, replace local law or authorise the assistant to make a final legal, medical, tax, immigration or disciplinary determination. Where this policy conflicts with a mandatory local requirement, the matter must be escalated to an authorised HR professional before action is taken.

Roles and accountability

Employees are responsible for giving complete and accurate information, reading the applicable procedure and obtaining required approvals before relying on an outcome. Managers are responsible for operational coverage, consistency and timely decisions. HR owns policy interpretation and exception records. Information Security owns technical access controls. Finance owns expense controls. Legal, tax and immigration specialists provide advice when a request crosses their areas. The MSAIE assistant may retrieve evidence, call approved tools, identify missing information and prepare fictional drafts, but it must not silently bypass an approval or claim that a provisional result is final.

Records and privacy

Only the minimum information needed for the stated purpose should be collected. Records must be stored in the approved synthetic system, labelled with the employee ID, action type, date and decision owner, and retained according to the records policy. Sensitive information must not be copied into free-text fields unless necessary. Access is role based and logs should show which tool was called, which arguments were supplied and what structured result was returned. Hidden chain-of-thought is not a business record and must not be exposed; concise operational traces are sufficient.

Exceptions and escalation

An exception requires a documented business reason, a named decision owner, a defined duration and the approvals listed in the manager approval matrix. A request must be escalated when facts are missing, evidence conflicts, a sensitive allegation is raised, the employee record cannot be found, the requested action would create a real external effect, or the policy corpus does not support a reliable answer. The assistant should say what is missing and should not fill gaps with invented facts. Emergency situations should use the company's emergency channels rather than the normal request workflow.

Review and assurance

HR reviews this fictional policy set annually and after any material incident or workflow failure. Quality checks sample citations, tool selections, confirmation gates and action logs. Findings are converted into corrective actions with an owner and target date. Evaluation results should include groundedness, citation accuracy, workflow completion, escalation accuracy, action safety and latency. Changes to a policy require version control, approval, an effective date and re-indexing of the retrieval store so that superseded text is not presented as current guidance.

Collection and classification

Before collecting an employee fact, the owner identifies the business purpose, authoritative source, permitted users and retention category. Forms and workflows collect only fields needed to complete that purpose and explain where a person can ask for correction. Sensitive data should be separated from routine administrative records where the approved system supports it. Free-text fields are not a substitute for a controlled field or confidential case system. Synthetic training fixtures are visibly labelled and kept distinct from live systems. The assistant receives only the fictional records configured for this project and must not infer or request additional private data to make an answer appear complete.

Access review and correction

Access follows role and work purpose, is logged and is reviewed when responsibilities change. The record owner removes access when it is no longer needed and investigates unexpected access through the security process. An employee correction request identifies the disputed field and the proposed correction; the owner records the evidence, outcome, effective date and reviewer. The original value remains available through the approved audit history unless an authorised retention or privacy process requires otherwise. A tool lookup can be incomplete or stale, so the assistant reports the field and source rather than treating it as a complete legal record.

Retention, legal hold and disposal

Each record type follows its approved retention schedule. A legal hold, investigation or mandatory requirement suspends ordinary disposal until the authorised owner releases it. Destruction is secure, documented and limited to the records covered by the instruction. Temporary local files, including synthetic mock-action logs and regenerated vector indexes, are handled under the demonstration's own retention and reset procedure; their deletion does not delete a real HR record. Do not keep duplicate exports or personal copies for convenience. When a request concerns a legal hold, retention exception or real employee file, refer it to the records owner and do not offer a deadline or outcome without source support.

AI-generated answers and operational traces

AI-assisted work may retain retrieved citation identifiers, tool names, validated arguments, structured results, confirmation decisions and the final answer needed to reconstruct the workflow. Hidden reasoning is not a business record and must not be exposed or logged as user-visible content. Logs omit credentials, unnecessary private data and raw provider secrets. A model can rewrite a controlled answer but does not become the source of truth; structured systems and cited policies remain authoritative. Evaluation cases use synthetic data, report their limits and are not merged into operational statistics. If the system cannot preserve a required privacy boundary, it should stop and report a safe failure rather than return unrefined text.
