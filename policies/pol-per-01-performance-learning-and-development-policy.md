# Performance, Learning and Development Policy

## Objectives and feedback

Managers and employees agree clear objectives and discuss progress regularly. Feedback should be specific, timely and connected to observable work. The assistant may help retrieve process guidance but must not generate a final performance rating or present an automated assessment as a manager decision.

## Development planning

Employees may identify skills, learning activities and career goals. Approval depends on relevance, cost and operational capacity. A development plan should include an outcome, owner and review date. Equal access should be considered, and decisions should not rely on protected characteristics.

## Performance concerns

Concerns should be raised early, with examples and an opportunity for the employee to respond. Formal action requires HR involvement and the applicable local process. The assistant must not diagnose motivation, infer misconduct or recommend dismissal from a short prompt. Sensitive allegations are escalated under the conduct policy.

## Records

Performance records should distinguish facts, feedback and decisions, and should be accessible only to authorised roles. Drafts generated by an AI tool must be reviewed for accuracy, tone and bias. The human manager remains accountable for the communication.

## Purpose and scope

This fictional policy forms part of MSAIE Group's demonstration HR governance framework. It applies only to the synthetic employees, managers, locations, balances and actions included in this educational project. The policy is designed to support consistent decisions, provide clear approval paths and preserve an auditable record. It does not create employment rights, replace local law or authorise the assistant to make a final legal, medical, tax, immigration or disciplinary determination. Where this policy conflicts with a mandatory local requirement, the matter must be escalated to an authorised HR professional before action is taken.

## Roles and accountability

Employees are responsible for giving complete and accurate information, reading the applicable procedure and obtaining required approvals before relying on an outcome. Managers are responsible for operational coverage, consistency and timely decisions. HR owns policy interpretation and exception records. Information Security owns technical access controls. Finance owns expense controls. Legal, tax and immigration specialists provide advice when a request crosses their areas. The MSAIE assistant may retrieve evidence, call approved tools, identify missing information and prepare fictional drafts, but it must not silently bypass an approval or claim that a provisional result is final.

## Records and privacy

Only the minimum information needed for the stated purpose should be collected. Records must be stored in the approved synthetic system, labelled with the employee ID, action type, date and decision owner, and retained according to the records policy. Sensitive information must not be copied into free-text fields unless necessary. Access is role based and logs should show which tool was called, which arguments were supplied and what structured result was returned. Hidden chain-of-thought is not a business record and must not be exposed; concise operational traces are sufficient.

## Exceptions and escalation

An exception requires a documented business reason, a named decision owner, a defined duration and the approvals listed in the manager approval matrix. A request must be escalated when facts are missing, evidence conflicts, a sensitive allegation is raised, the employee record cannot be found, the requested action would create a real external effect, or the policy corpus does not support a reliable answer. The assistant should say what is missing and should not fill gaps with invented facts. Emergency situations should use the company's emergency channels rather than the normal request workflow.

## Review and assurance

HR reviews this fictional policy set annually and after any material incident or workflow failure. Quality checks sample citations, tool selections, confirmation gates and action logs. Findings are converted into corrective actions with an owner and target date. Evaluation results should include groundedness, citation accuracy, workflow completion, escalation accuracy, action safety and latency. Changes to a policy require version control, approval, an effective date and re-indexing of the retrieval store so that superseded text is not presented as current guidance.

## Goal setting and review conversations

Goals should describe the expected outcome, relevant measure, support available and review date. Employees and managers agree on priorities that fit the role and record material changes when business needs shift. A check-in is a two-way conversation: the employee can raise unclear expectations, obstacles, workload or needed support. Managers distinguish observed work from interpretation and use examples relevant to the stated goal. The assistant may explain this process or help structure a neutral agenda, but must not infer performance from a single message, sentiment, productivity signal or private record.

## Feedback and development support

Feedback should be timely, specific, respectful and connected to work expectations. Development plans can include mentoring, training, practice assignments, documented support and a follow-up conversation. The employee and manager identify who owns each action and how completion will be reviewed. Learning options do not promise promotion, compensation or role change. If an employee requests a course or equipment to support learning, the relevant budget and technology approvals still apply. The assistant can help prepare a neutral draft when asked and confirmed, and must preserve the employee's own wording where a quotation is requested.

## Performance concerns and support plans

When work is below a stated expectation, the manager describes the requirement, the specific observed gap, its work impact, the support offered and the next review point. The employee has an opportunity to provide context and correct factual errors. A support plan is not a disciplinary finding or a promise of a particular outcome. HR should review material or contested action, and an allegation involving discrimination, harassment, health or retaliation follows the confidential escalation policy. The assistant must not diagnose a cause, decide credibility or recommend a high-impact employment action. It should direct the user to HR when a request concerns a real employee decision.

## Calibration, records and appeal

Managers apply role-relevant criteria consistently across comparable work and explain material differences in expectations. Calibration uses the minimum information needed and restricts access to authorised decision-makers. Notes distinguish the employee's statement, observable evidence and the manager's conclusion. An employee who identifies a factual error may request correction or review through HR; the assistant cannot adjudicate an appeal or promise a result. Retain review materials under the records schedule and apply a legal hold when instructed. A confirmed mock ticket may capture a neutral follow-up request but is not a confidential case-management system and must not contain unnecessary sensitive detail.
