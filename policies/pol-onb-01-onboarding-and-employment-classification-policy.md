# Onboarding and Employment Classification Policy

## Classification

Every synthetic worker is recorded as full-time, part-time, fixed-term, intern or contractor. Classification determines eligibility for PTO, benefits and some flexible-work arrangements. Managers must not promise employee benefits to a contractor or change a classification through an informal message. HR owns the classification record and any correction.

## Onboarding controls

Before access is granted, the worker must have a recorded manager, start date, office or remote location, required agreements and completed security induction. Access should be limited to the role and reviewed after the initial period. Missing data must be corrected rather than inferred. The assistant may retrieve the profile but should not expose fields unrelated to the user's legitimate question.

## Probation and service

A fictional probation period may apply, but it does not remove legal rights or the duty to act fairly. Service is calculated from the recorded start date and is used by several policies, including international remote work. Breaks in service or acquisitions require HR review. The assistant should use the structured months-of-service field and avoid calculating from incomplete dates when a reliable field is available.

## Role changes and departures

Transfers, promotions and departures require updates to access, manager, location and benefit records. Departing personnel must return equipment and lose access at the authorised time. Records needed for payroll, compliance or disputes are retained according to policy. A tool failure must not result in continued access or an assumed status.

## Purpose and scope

This fictional policy forms part of MSAIE Group's demonstration HR governance framework. It applies only to the synthetic employees, managers, locations, balances and actions included in this educational project. The policy is designed to support consistent decisions, provide clear approval paths and preserve an auditable record. It does not create employment rights, replace local law or authorise the assistant to make a final legal, medical, tax, immigration or disciplinary determination. Where this policy conflicts with a mandatory local requirement, the matter must be escalated to an authorised HR professional before action is taken.

## Roles and accountability

Employees are responsible for giving complete and accurate information, reading the applicable procedure and obtaining required approvals before relying on an outcome. Managers are responsible for operational coverage, consistency and timely decisions. HR owns policy interpretation and exception records. Information Security owns technical access controls. Finance owns expense controls. Legal, tax and immigration specialists provide advice when a request crosses their areas. The MSAIE assistant may retrieve evidence, call approved tools, identify missing information and prepare fictional drafts, but it must not silently bypass an approval or claim that a provisional result is final.

## Records and privacy

Only the minimum information needed for the stated purpose should be collected. Records must be stored in the approved synthetic system, labelled with the employee ID, action type, date and decision owner, and retained according to the records policy. Sensitive information must not be copied into free-text fields unless necessary. Access is role based and logs should show which tool was called, which arguments were supplied and what structured result was returned. Hidden chain-of-thought is not a business record and must not be exposed; concise operational traces are sufficient.

## Exceptions and escalation

An exception requires a documented business reason, a named decision owner, a defined duration and the approvals listed in the manager approval matrix. A request must be escalated when facts are missing, evidence conflicts, a sensitive allegation is raised, the employee record cannot be found, the requested action would create a real external effect, or the policy corpus does not support a reliable answer. The assistant should say what is missing and should not fill gaps with invented facts. Emergency situations should use the company's emergency channels rather than the normal request workflow.

## Review and assurance

HR reviews this fictional policy set annually and after any material incident or workflow failure. Quality checks sample citations, tool selections, confirmation gates and action logs. Findings are converted into corrective actions with an owner and target date. Evaluation results should include groundedness, citation accuracy, workflow completion, escalation accuracy, action safety and latency. Changes to a policy require version control, approval, an effective date and re-indexing of the retrieval store so that superseded text is not presented as current guidance.

## Pre-start coordination

Before a start date, the manager confirms the role, reporting line, work location, planned duties and support needed for the first weeks. HR owns the authoritative employment classification and start-date record; IT provisions only access approved for the role. Equipment, training, benefits orientation and work arrangements should have a named owner and target date. The assistant may present a checklist but cannot confirm that a background review, identity check, contract signature or access grant occurred unless a structured record explicitly reports it. Do not place identity documents or sensitive screening results in a free-text HR ticket.

## Classification and record conflicts

Classification depends on the approved contract and recorded facts, not on a job title alone. Weekly hours, contract type, start date, duties and work location may affect which policy applies. If an employee's account differs from the HR record, the assistant should state which synthetic field it retrieved and refer the discrepancy to HR. It must not infer that a contractor is eligible for employee plans, convert part-time hours into a benefit determination or alter a classification. A correction request identifies the field, reason, evidence source and authorised reviewer. The existing record remains the source for automated workflows until HR records a change.

## First-week orientation and access

Managers should explain core responsibilities, team contacts, safety channels, equipment care, data handling, attendance expectations and where employees can find current procedures. Required learning is tracked by the learning system, not by the assistant's display of a policy excerpt. Access follows least privilege and is removed or adjusted when the role changes. Employees should know how to report an accommodation need, workplace concern or urgent safety issue without placing sensitive detail in a general checklist. A missing access or equipment item may be routed through support, but a local mock ticket does not provision an account or fulfil training.

## Status changes and employee privacy

A change to employment type, hours, manager, location, duties or contract end date should be recorded by HR and communicated to affected system owners. The effective date matters: a future change is not active early, and a past change is not backdated without an authorised correction. Managers coordinate work handover and equipment return; IT reviews access; HR reviews effects on benefits, leave and policy eligibility. An accommodation or protected-leave question uses the confidential HR process. The assistant may explain the route but cannot determine legal status, work authorisation or contractual rights. A successful lookup shows that a fixture exists, not that a real employee has completed onboarding.
