# International Remote Work Policy

## Eligibility

International remote work is available to eligible full-time and part-time synthetic employees who have completed at least six months of service. Contractors require a separate contract review. The normal maximum is twenty calendar days in a rolling twelve-month period, including weekends and public holidays that fall within the approved overseas period. The current rolling usage must be retrieved from the employee profile before a provisional decision is made.

## Required reviews

Every international request requires manager, HR, tax, information-security and immigration review before travel. The manager confirms duties and coverage; HR confirms classification and policy consistency; tax reviews payroll and permanent-establishment exposure; information security reviews data and access; immigration confirms that the planned activity is permitted. The assistant may identify the approvals, but it must not represent specialist review as completed unless the structured record explicitly says so.

## Location and duration controls

The destination must be named before final approval. Access from a sanctioned, prohibited or high-risk location is not allowed. A request that would take the rolling total above twenty days is not ordinarily eligible and must be escalated rather than split into artificial segments. Employees must provide the intended dates, destination, work activities and contact details. Personal travel before or after the approved period is outside the work approval unless separately documented.

## Working arrangements

Normal working hours, availability expectations and performance requirements continue unless the manager approves an alternative. The employee must have a safe workspace and a reliable connection, and must comply with local health, safety and emergency instructions. The company does not reimburse ordinary personal travel or accommodation unless another policy and written approval applies. The provisional response should distinguish policy eligibility from final travel authorisation.

## Purpose and scope

This fictional policy forms part of MSAIE Group's demonstration HR governance framework. It applies only to the synthetic employees, managers, locations, balances and actions included in this educational project. The policy is designed to support consistent decisions, provide clear approval paths and preserve an auditable record. It does not create employment rights, replace local law or authorise the assistant to make a final legal, medical, tax, immigration or disciplinary determination. Where this policy conflicts with a mandatory local requirement, the matter must be escalated to an authorised HR professional before action is taken.

## Roles and accountability

Employees are responsible for giving complete and accurate information, reading the applicable procedure and obtaining required approvals before relying on an outcome. Managers are responsible for operational coverage, consistency and timely decisions. HR owns policy interpretation and exception records. Information Security owns technical access controls. Finance owns expense controls. Legal, tax and immigration specialists provide advice when a request crosses their areas. The MSAIE assistant may retrieve evidence, call approved tools, identify missing information and prepare fictional drafts, but it must not silently bypass an approval or claim that a provisional result is final.

## Records and privacy

Only the minimum information needed for the stated purpose should be collected. Records must be stored in the approved synthetic system, labelled with the employee ID, action type, date and decision owner, and retained according to the records policy. Sensitive information must not be copied into free-text fields unless necessary. Access is role based and logs should show which tool was called, which arguments were supplied and what structured result was returned. Hidden chain-of-thought is not a business record and must not be exposed; concise operational traces are sufficient.

## Exceptions and escalation

An exception requires a documented business reason, a named decision owner, a defined duration and the approvals listed in the manager approval matrix. A request must be escalated when facts are missing, evidence conflicts, a sensitive allegation is raised, the employee record cannot be found, the requested action would create a real external effect, or the policy corpus does not support a reliable answer. The assistant should say what is missing and should not fill gaps with invented facts. Emergency situations should use the company's emergency channels rather than the normal request workflow.

## Review and assurance

HR reviews this fictional policy set annually and after any material incident or workflow failure. Quality checks sample citations, tool selections, confirmation gates and action logs. Findings are converted into corrective actions with an owner and target date. Evaluation results should include groundedness, citation accuracy, workflow completion, escalation accuracy, action safety and latency. Changes to a policy require version control, approval, an effective date and re-indexing of the retrieval store so that superseded text is not presented as current guidance.

## Request intake and provisional assessment

Before a provisional review, collect the synthetic employee ID, destination country, intended dates, work activities, requested calendar days and current rolling usage from the employee profile. If the destination or duration is missing, ask for it instead of implying that all locations are treated alike. The assistant may calculate the rolling total from returned usage and requested days, then compare it with the policy limit. That calculation is an eligibility screen only. It does not reserve an allowance, verify border requirements or authorise travel. The response states the total, relevant limit and missing information without claiming that required reviews have already occurred.

## Specialist review sequence

The manager first checks duties, coverage and timing. HR checks classification and policy consistency. Tax reviews payroll and permanent-establishment exposure; Information Security reviews destination, data and access; Immigration reviews whether the intended activity is permitted. Each specialist records a decision and any conditions in the authorised system. One person's approval does not stand in for another role's review. If a specialist identifies a restriction or requests more information, the request remains pending until its owner resolves it. The assistant can enumerate required reviews and summarize recorded statuses only when a structured source provides them.

## Destination, data and day counting

The approved destination and dates define the scope of an international-work decision. The period is counted in calendar days, including weekends and public holidays that fall within the approved overseas period. Splitting one continuous stay into separate requests does not reset the rolling window. Personal travel outside the work period is not covered by work approval. A change of country, work activity, duration, device, data class or travel date may change tax, immigration, security or coverage risk and requires reassessment. The assistant must not characterize the request as finally approved if a required review is incomplete.

## Safe setup, extensions and return

Before departure, the employee confirms an appropriate workspace, reliable connectivity, work-hour expectations, emergency contacts and access to approved collaboration tools. Company information is handled under the security policy, including restrictions on sensitive data and public environments. An extension or date change is submitted before the approved period ends and reviewed against remaining rolling usage and destination requirements. A previous decision is not automatically renewed, and unused days do not guarantee future approval. If an incident occurs abroad, use the applicable security, safety or emergency route. The assistant may list follow-up items but cannot record specialist approval, travel booking or immigration outcome without a structured source.
