# Business Expense and Travel Reimbursement Policy

## Allowable expenses

Reasonable, necessary and properly approved business expenses may be reimbursed. Personal purchases, fines, upgrades for personal preference and expenses already paid by another party are not reimbursable. International travel requires the approvals in the manager approval matrix before booking. The lowest logical fare and a safe, practical itinerary should be selected unless an approved exception applies.

## Receipts and submission

Itemised receipts are required for expenses of twenty-five dollars or more. Claims should be submitted within thirty calendar days of the transaction or return from travel. The employee must identify the business purpose, attendees where relevant, currency and project code. A missing receipt requires a written explanation and manager approval; repeated missing receipts are referred to Finance.

## Currency and taxes

Foreign-currency claims use the card statement amount or the approved conversion method. Recoverable taxes should be identified where the system supports it. The assistant may explain the rule but must not give personal tax advice. A claim involving relocation, extended overseas work or a potential taxable benefit requires specialist review.

## Controls and audit

Managers check necessity, coding and policy compliance. Finance may sample claims, request clarification or reject unsupported items. Employees must not split a transaction to avoid a receipt or approval threshold. Records are retained under the records policy. A mock ticket can capture a disputed claim after confirmation, but no payment or accounting entry is created.

## Purpose and scope

This fictional policy forms part of MSAIE Group's demonstration HR governance framework. It applies only to the synthetic employees, managers, locations, balances and actions included in this educational project. The policy is designed to support consistent decisions, provide clear approval paths and preserve an auditable record. It does not create employment rights, replace local law or authorise the assistant to make a final legal, medical, tax, immigration or disciplinary determination. Where this policy conflicts with a mandatory local requirement, the matter must be escalated to an authorised HR professional before action is taken.

## Roles and accountability

Employees are responsible for giving complete and accurate information, reading the applicable procedure and obtaining required approvals before relying on an outcome. Managers are responsible for operational coverage, consistency and timely decisions. HR owns policy interpretation and exception records. Information Security owns technical access controls. Finance owns expense controls. Legal, tax and immigration specialists provide advice when a request crosses their areas. The MSAIE assistant may retrieve evidence, call approved tools, identify missing information and prepare fictional drafts, but it must not silently bypass an approval or claim that a provisional result is final.

## Records and privacy

Only the minimum information needed for the stated purpose should be collected. Records must be stored in the approved synthetic system, labelled with the employee ID, action type, date and decision owner, and retained according to the records policy. Sensitive information must not be copied into free-text fields unless necessary. Access is role based and logs should show which tool was called, which arguments were supplied and what structured result was returned. Hidden chain-of-thought is not a business record and must not be exposed; concise operational traces are sufficient.

## Exceptions and escalation

An exception requires a documented business reason, a named decision owner, a defined duration and the approvals listed in the manager approval matrix. A request must be escalated when facts are missing, evidence conflicts, a sensitive allegation is raised, the employee record cannot be found, the requested action would create a real external effect, or the policy corpus does not support a reliable answer. The assistant should say what is missing and should not fill gaps with invented facts. Emergency situations should use the company's emergency channels rather than the normal request workflow.

## Review and assurance

HR reviews this fictional policy set annually and after any material incident or workflow failure. Quality checks sample citations, tool selections, confirmation gates and action logs. Findings are converted into corrective actions with an owner and target date. Evaluation results should include groundedness, citation accuracy, workflow completion, escalation accuracy, action safety and latency. Changes to a policy require version control, approval, an effective date and re-indexing of the retrieval store so that superseded text is not presented as current guidance.

## Before booking business travel

The employee submits the business purpose, destination, travel dates, expected cost, funding code and any operational constraint before making a non-refundable booking. The manager checks business need, coverage and budget; Finance reviews an exception or unusual cost; specialist reviews are added when the itinerary involves overseas work, restricted data or a potential tax concern. A provisional itinerary is not travel authorisation. Employees should compare reasonable options using the approved booking route and explain a higher-cost choice, such as a schedule limitation or accessibility requirement, in the request. The assistant can list required approvals but cannot reserve travel, approve spend or represent a specialist review as complete.

## Claim preparation and evidence

Each claim should connect the expense to an approved business activity and identify the transaction date, currency, merchant, amount, project or cost code and payment method. Itemised receipts should show what was purchased; a card slip alone may not distinguish a business item from a personal one. For a group meal, record the business purpose and attendees where appropriate without adding sensitive employee detail. If a receipt is unavailable, explain why and submit the exception for manager review. Do not reconstruct a receipt, alter a date or divide one purchase into smaller claims to avoid a control. Finance may return incomplete claims for correction.

## Changes, cancellations and exceptions

When dates, destination, traveller or work purpose changes after approval, notify the manager before incurring additional cost. Material changes may require the request and specialist reviews to be reassessed. A cancelled booking should be documented with the supplier credit or refund outcome so it is not claimed as a new expense. Personal extensions, companion costs and upgrades remain outside the business claim unless the applicable policy and a written exception say otherwise. An itinerary change does not silently amend an international-work approval. If the new destination changes security, tax or immigration risk, pause and obtain the relevant review before travelling.

## Review, payment and privacy

The manager checks that the claim matches the stated business purpose and approved travel; Finance checks evidence, arithmetic, currency conversion and duplicate reimbursement. A reviewer should return a claim with a clear reason and the missing evidence rather than editing the employee's statement without notice. An employee who disputes a decision may request a documented review through Finance. A submitted claim, local mock ticket or draft email does not prove that money was paid. Do not paste full card numbers, security codes, medical information or unrelated details into HR notes. Cross-border work, relocation and tax questions go to the appropriate specialist rather than being answered as personal tax advice.
